## ground-state
### instruction
Start at n = 1, the ground state, then set n to 2. Compare the energy and the green probability-density peaks for the two states.

### hint
Select 2 below the plot, or move the n control to 2. The green density changes from one peak to two; the blue curve is the wave function.

### insight
The green curve is |ψ|², the probability density; the blue wave function can also be negative. Moving from n = 1 to n = 2 adds an interior node and raises the energy by a factor of four at the same box width.

## increase-n
### instruction
Increase n to 3. Count the interior nodes — places where the wave function and probability density are zero.

### hint
Select 3 or move n to 3. There are two interior nodes separating three probability-density peaks.

### insight
The nth state has n − 1 interior nodes and n probability-density peaks. At fixed box width its energy scales as n², so n = 3 has nine times the ground-state energy.

## shrink-box
### instruction
Now decrease box width L to 0.5. Watch the energy levels spread apart as the particle is confined to less space.

### hint
Move L to 0.5 using its control or drag the right box wall left. Compare energies at the same n.

### insight
This is the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle in action! Confining the particle to a smaller space (knowing its position better) forces it to have higher energy (more uncertain momentum). Energy scales as 1/L squared. This is why atoms don't collapse -- squeezing electrons closer to the nucleus would cost too much energy.
